### Solar Power
[Cincinnati Solar Power](https://www.cincinnati-oh.gov/oes/buildings-and-energy/cincinnati-solar-project/)

[It's illegal to join a community solar program in Ohio](https://www.ohiocitizen.org/community_solar)

I don't understand why Cincinnati didn't actually purchase the Solar Plant they are getting energy from, but they had a private company develop it and took out a PPA (Power Purchase Agreement). It's still an admirable development! The City should invest in more solar and own the plants. Ideally, the city could provide free power to itself and residents, while charging private organizations for energy usage.

Alternatively, they could place new investments in wind power, which Ohio is well suited for. The infrastructure certainly doesn't need to be close by (the solar plant Cincinnati made it's PPA with is in Highland County).

### Subway
> Cincinnati owns a buried concrete tunnel approximately 2 miles long originally built in the mid-1920s for rail transit (a subway tunnel). However, the tunnel has never been utilized for that purpose. The tunnel is situated beneath Central Parkway, an arterial street in central Cincinnati beginning in the downtown neighborhood near Walnut Street and ending just north of the Western Hills Viaduct/McMillan Street intersection with Central Parkway just east of Interstate 75. The City is seeking conceptual proposals to identify potential firms and gather ideas for utilizing the tunnel for a commercial or other purpose. I am currently seeking public planners, civil engineers, transportation specialists, architects, and others to help draft a proposal to repair the tunnels and create an operational subway system in the heart of downtown. The short term plan would be to extend the subway along the interstate into the northern suburb of Cincinnati to help address ongoing housing and transportation issues. In the future, this could serve as an infrastructure platform for a city wide loop using the same basic principles of construction. Proposals are due on Oct 17th 2024. 

[Brief article](https://www.cincinnati.com/story/news/politics/2024/10/02/city-of-cincinnati-seeking-ideas-to-repurpose-empty-subway-tunnel/75486296007/)

[At least one city council member is open to a subway, but funding is the largest issue](https://www.wcpo.com/news/local-news/i-would-love-a-regional-subway-system-city-leaders-discuss-options-for-cincinnatis-abandoned-tunnel)

[Revival has been attempted *many* times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cincinnati_Subway#Attempts_at_revival)

[FTA Grants](https://www.transit.dot.gov/funding/grants/better-utilizing-investments-leverage-development-build-transportation-grants-program)

There is someone in the Majority Report Discord who is trying to create a proposal for this tunnel. Whether that proposal gains traction or not, the city seems to be planning to act on this in a little time. There is currently a water main and fiber optic infrastructure running through the tunnel, but it will need to be removed at some point in the future and that is when they want to use the tunnel for another purpose. It could be possible for some kind of organizing to promote this proposal or another like it to utilize our public infrastructure for it's intended purpose.

Whether funded through an additional tax levy or federal grants, this could be a huge investment in Cincinnati. It would improve transit for underserved communities, reduce traffic, and position Cincinnati for future growth.

### Local Campaign Finance Reform
> In `Citizens United vs FEC` The Supreme Court held 5–4 that the freedom of speech clause of the First Amendment prohibits the government from restricting independent expenditures for political campaigns by corporations, nonprofit organizations, labor unions, and other associations.

Passing local laws regarding campaign finance reform in violation of this decision would be subject to the Supreme Court's decision, but the test case could be appealed all the way up to the Supreme Court. I believe this could be a local law too, it wouldn't need to be statewide. It may be unrealistic, but if DSA were to lead in passing a law like this which started a legal battle moving up towards the Supreme Court, we would be in a good position (as the initial proponents of the law) to lead a coalition effort.
